**Course implications [Judgment, from verified facts].**
- Wind is the dominant bike variable (open North Texas roads; 2026 experience). Pace by power/HR, never by speed; practise aero position and long steady efforts.
- ~1,400 ft over 56 mi is modest: sustained power and aerodynamics matter more than climbing-specific power; the skill is controlling power on gradual rises (no surges).

- Cool water/air early, upper-70s–80s °F by the run: plan for both cold-start swim management and run heat.
- A shortened or cancelled swim is possible, but you cannot plan for it — prepare for the full 1.9 km.

**Objective view of Waco.** Without splits, no one can determine *how much* pacing cost you. What can be said:
- A 5:20 **without a swim** is not a full-triathlon benchmark. Adding a ~35–42 min swim and a longer T1 means bike + T2 + run must be roughly **45–50 min faster** than at Waco for sub-5:15.
- That is a large improvement in one cycle. It is plausible **only if** (a) the Waco run was heavily compromised (e.g., bike over-pacing → late-run walking, heat), and (b) bike fitness rises meaningfully. Dallas's flatter run and spring temperatures may help relative to an October race in Waco — but wind can make the bike slower.
- Your belief that pacing cost you time is reasonable and common in second 70.3s, but it is a hypothesis until tested. **Enter Waco splits, average/normalized power and run HR on the Profile page**; the app then compares your Waco bike intensity and run fade against these models.

## 4. Evidence base (peer-reviewed and position stands)

Format: **Citation** — URL · *Key findings* · *Limitations* · **→ Implication for your plan**.

### Intensity distribution & aerobic development
1. **Seiler S. (2010). What is best practice for training intensity and duration distribution in endurance athletes? _IJSPP_ 5(3):276–291.** — https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.5.3.276 · *Narrative review: well-trained endurance athletes accumulate most training at low intensity, with a smaller fraction at high intensity.* · *Largely observational, elite samples.* · **→ ~75–85% of weekly time easy/steady; 2–3 quality sessions per week; easy-day HR caps.**
2. **Seiler KS, Kjerland GØ. (2006). Quantifying training intensity distribution in elite endurance athletes: is there evidence for an "optimal" distribution? _Scand J Med Sci Sports_ 16:49–56.** — https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0838.2004.00418.x · *Junior elite skiers' training was dominated by low intensity.* · *Small elite sample; one sport.* · **→ Supports low-intensity volume as the base.**
3. **Stöggl T, Sperlich B. (2014). Polarized training has greater impact on key endurance variables than threshold, high intensity, or high volume training. _Front Physiol_ 5:33.** — https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2014.00033 · *9-week intervention; polarized group improved more on key endurance variables than threshold, HIT, or high-volume groups.* · *Small groups, short duration.* · **→ Keep quality focused and easy days easy.**
4. **Neal CM et al. (2013). Six weeks of a polarized training-intensity distribution leads to greater physiological and performance adaptations than a threshold model in trained cyclists. _J Appl Physiol_ 114:461–471.** — https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.00652.2012 · *Polarized > threshold-dominant over 6 weeks in trained cyclists.* · *Short, small.* · **→ Threshold work is dosed, not the default.**
5. **Rosenblat MA et al. (2019). Polarized vs. threshold training intensity distribution on endurance sport performance: systematic review and meta-analysis. _J Strength Cond Res_ 33:3491–3500.** — https://doi.org/10.1519/jsc.0000000000002618 · *Pooled data favored polarized over threshold for time-trial performance, with uncertainty.* · *Few, small RCTs.* · **→ Polarized principles, applied pragmatically.**
6. **Casado A et al. (2022). Training periodization, methods, intensity distribution, and volume in highly trained and elite distance runners: a systematic review. _IJSPP_ 17:820–833.** — https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.2021-0435 · *Elite runners use high low-intensity volume with pyramidal or polarized distributions; threshold work is common.* · *Elite runners, not triathletes.* · **→ Pyramidal base (sweet spot/tempo) is legitimate; threshold run work every 1–2 weeks.**
7. **Laursen PB. (2010). Training for intense exercise performance: high-intensity or high-volume training? _Scand J Med Sci Sports_ 20:1–10.** — https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0838.2010.01184.x · *Both volume and HIT contribute; HIT can substitute partially when time is limited.* · **→ Short VO₂ blocks (Build 1) when weekday time is limited.**

### Thresholds, critical power/speed, CSS
8. **Jones AM, Vanhatalo A. (2017). The 'critical power' concept: applications to sports performance with a focus on intermittent high-intensity exercise. _Sports Med_ 47:65–78.** — https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-017-0688-0 · *CP/CS marks the boundary between heavy and severe domains; useful for prescribing intervals.* · **→ Threshold sessions target the heavy-severe boundary; race pace sits below it.**
9. **Poole DC et al. (2016). Critical power: an important fatigue threshold in exercise physiology. _MSSE_ 48:2320–2334.** — https://doi.org/10.1249/mss.0000000000000939 · *Exercise above CP leads to progressive fatigue; below CP is sustainable longer.* · **→ Pacing above threshold on rises "borrows" from the run; bike ceiling ≈ FTP.**
10. **Jones AM et al. (2019). The maximal metabolic steady state: redefining the 'gold standard'. _Physiol Rep_ 7:e14098.** — https://doi.org/10.14814/phy2.14098 · *CP is a better index of maximal metabolic steady state than MLSS for many purposes.* · **→ FTP/threshold tests are practical proxies; retest rather than assume.**
11. **Wakayoshi K et al. (1992). Determination and validity of critical velocity as an index of swimming performance in the competitive swimmer. _Eur J Appl Physiol_ 64:153–157.** — https://doi.org/10.1007/bf00717953 · *Critical swimming velocity correlates with endurance performance.* · **→ CSS test (400 + 200) anchors swim zones.**
12. **Dekerle J et al. (2005). Critical swimming speed does not represent the speed at maximal lactate steady state. _Int J Sports Med_ 26:524–530.** — https://doi.org/10.1055/s-2004-821227 · *CSS tends to sit above MLSS speed.* · **→ CSS sets are threshold work (hard); race effort = CSS +3–6 s/100.**
13. **Martin L et al. (2000). Comparison of critical swimming velocity and velocity at lactate threshold in elite triathletes. _Int J Sports Med_ 21:366–368.** — https://doi.org/10.1055/s-2000-3786 · *CSS relates closely to lactate-threshold velocity in triathletes.* · **→ CSS is valid for triathletes.**

21. **Barnes KR, Kilding AE. (2015). Strategies to improve running economy. _Sports Med_ 45:37–56.** — https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-014-0246-y · *Strength, plyometric and specific training can improve economy.* · **→ Hills + strides + heavy lifting.**
22. **Lauersen JB et al. (2014). The effectiveness of exercise interventions to prevent sports injuries: systematic review and meta-analysis. _Br J Sports Med_ 48:871–877.** — https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2013-092538 · *Strength training reduced sports injuries; stretching did not show a protective effect.* · **→ Strength twice weekly is also injury prevention; mobility is optional.**

## 5. Plan design — how evidence became your schedule

**Structure (23 weeks).** W0 post-race recovery → W1–2 transition → **Base 1** W3–7 (baseline tests, sweet spot, hills, technique, heavy strength) → **Base 2** W8–11 (threshold bike + seated climbing, threshold runs, longer long runs) → **Build 1** W12–15 (VO₂ + threshold sharpening, race-power long-ride blocks, longer bricks) → **Build 2** W16–19 (rolling-terrain over-unders, 80–90+ min race power with full fueling, race-pace long runs, open-water skills) → **Peak** W20 (race simulation) → **Taper** W21 → **Race week** W22.

**Weekly template [Judgment].** Mon rest · Tue bike quality + strength A · Wed CSS swim + easy run · Thu run quality + strength B · Fri swim technique/endurance/open-water skills · Sat long ride + brick · Sun long run. Hard leg days (Tue/Thu/Sat) are never back-to-back on weekdays (tested in code); swim quality sits between them because swimming spares the legs. Lifting follows the endurance session on hard days so easy days stay easy (#18).

**Volume.** ~6 h (transition) → ~9–11 h (base) → ~11–12 h (build) → ~9 h (peak) → ~7.7 h (taper) → ~3 h + race. Long ride peaks at 3:30 with up to 90 min at race power; long run peaks at 105 min; brick runs progress 10 → 45 min.

**Deloads (3:1).** W7 −47%, W11 −53%, W15 −46%, W19 −45% vs. the preceding week; short intensity retained; lifting sets halved with load ~90%; tests at the end when fresh. Larger cuts follow higher-density blocks and holiday disruption (Thanksgiving, Christmas). **[Judgment informed by #31–33]**

**Taper.** ~2 weeks: −34% (W21), then ≈ −70% before the race (W22), intensity and frequency maintained (#32–35).

**Zones & targets.** Bike: % of validated FTP (Coggan levels) with Friel bike-LTHR and RPE alternatives. Run: multiples of threshold pace (30-min TT), Friel run-LTHR, RPE. Swim: CSS ± s/100. Until a benchmark exists, targets display as provisional RPE/HR guidance — no invented numbers. Zones change only from tests; trends require a change beyond test noise, and "confirmed" requires two consecutive tests in the same direction.

**Race execution (bike).** Race power 75–80% FTP (likely ~77%), first 15 min at 68–73%, ≤90% on short rises, ≤85% on long rises/headwind, ceiling = FTP, VI ≤1.05, final 20 min lighter. **[Judgment]**: common 70.3 guidance places IF around 0.75–0.85 depending on ability and bike duration; the lower half is chosen because the Waco lesson is to protect the run. Brick evidence will show whether you can go higher.

**Race execution (run).** Miles 0–2 10–15 s/mi slower than goal; effort-based on undulations; HR caps by segment; decision point at mile 10.

**Strength.** Re-introduction (W1–2) → baseline e1RM (W3) → strength development 4×5–6 @ RIR 2–3 (W4–10) → maintenance 3×4–5 (W12–14) → race-specific 2×3–5, one heavy + one short session (W16–19) → single sessions (W20–21) → none in race week. Exercises cover hinge, squat, single-leg, upper push/pull, calf and soleus, adductors (soccer history), anti-rotation trunk.

## 8. Body composition & nutrition — recommendation

At 6'0" / 183 lb with a strength background, there is no evidence-based reason to require weight loss for a sub-5:15 on a modestly rolling course; aerodynamics, sustainable power, durability and pacing matter more. **If** you want a change, limit it to the base phase (≤0.25–0.5% body weight per week), never under-fuel sessions, and hold weight steady from late December. Protein 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day; carbohydrate matched to the day; 60–90 g/h on long sessions; sweat-rate testing for fluid and sodium. No target weight is set (#43–54).
